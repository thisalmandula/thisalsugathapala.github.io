Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS), including LiDAR, radar, and optical cameras, play a crucial role in improving road safety and reducing traffic accidents, particularly under challenging weather conditions. However, heavy rain and snowfall can introduce environmental contamination that significantly degrades sensor performance. One important source of contamination is generated by the front wheels, which can transport water, snow, and road debris into the sensor’s field of view.

For my master’s thesis, I joined Petter Ekman’s research group developing numerical and experimental methods to improve the modelling and understanding of front-wheel-induced contamination and its impact on vehicle sensors. A key challenge in contamination modelling is understanding how different automotive surfaces, such as glass, plastic, rubber, and painted metal, affect the way water moves and forms liquid films. In this project, we developed and validated a numerical approach in STAR-CCM+ to better capture these surface effects and achieve good agreement between simulations and experimental measurements across a range of automotive materials.

**Team members:** Twan Bakker

**University supervisors (Linköping University):** Petter Ekman

**Industry supervisors (Volvo Group):** Torbjörn Virdung and Johan Forsgren

**Key Highlights &amp; Methodology**

<div class="project-highlights">
  <ul>
    <li>
      <strong>Dynamic Contact Angle Modeling:</strong> Performed a systematic parametric study in STAR-CCM+ and identified the Blended Kistler dynamic contact angle model as the most suitable approach for capturing material-dependent surface flow.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>High-Resolution LIF Experiments:</strong> Conducted droplet experiments using Light-Induced Fluorescence (LIF) imaging across two droplet sizes and five surface inclinations ($15^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $75^\circ$). The experiments captured static, advancing, and receding contact angles for model parameterization.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Real-Vehicle Surface Testing:</strong> Tested glass and painted metal samples taken directly from a Volvo S60 door panel, alongside standardized plastic and rubber surfaces, to investigate how real automotive materials affect water-film behaviour.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>VOF Rivulet Simulations:</strong> Conducted Volume of Fluid (VOF) multiphase simulations in STAR-CCM+ to model rivulet transport across all four materials at five inclination angles.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Experimental Validation:</strong> Compared numerical predictions with experimental rivulet dynamics and demonstrated that carefully calibrated surface properties can reproduce the water-flow behaviour observed on different real-world automotive materials.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>
